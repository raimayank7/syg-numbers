Roman numeral converter

Type a number from 1 to 3999, or a Roman numeral such as XLII. You get the other form and the way it is built.

How to use the converter

Type figures to get a Roman numeral, or letters to get a number. The result shows the numeral, the number in words and how the numeral is built from its parts, so that you can check your own working. It accepts only correctly formed numerals. If you type IIII or IC, it tells you so and does not guess, because a school answer in that form would be marked wrong.

The symbols at a glance

IVXLCDM
1510501005001000

Letters are added when a smaller one follows a larger one (VI is 6) and subtracted when a smaller one comes first (IV is 4). Only six subtractive pairs are allowed: IV, IX, XL, XC, CD and CM.

Why it stops at 3999

No letter may be written more than three times in a row, and there is no single letter above M. So the largest numeral in the standard form is MMMCMXCIX, which is 3999. There is also no Roman numeral for zero. Larger values were written by the Romans with a bar over a letter to multiply it by a thousand, which is beyond school work.

Good uses

  • Checking homework on Roman numerals.
  • Reading the year at the end of a film or on a foundation stone.
  • Writing chapter numbers, class numbers or a date in Roman numerals for a card or a design.
